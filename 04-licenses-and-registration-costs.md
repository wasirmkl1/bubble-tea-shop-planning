# Licenses, Registrations & Compliance Costs (Bangladesh)

This covers the government fees and compliance costs needed to legally run your bubble tea shop in Khulna — separate from the equipment/ingredient/rent costs already covered in earlier documents.

⚠️ **Important honesty check up front:** Bangladesh government fee schedules are not consistently published online in a clean, searchable format — much of what exists is scanned Bangla-language PDFs from local city corporation offices, which are hard to parse precisely even with search tools. The figures below are **best-effort estimates** compiled from available sources, general small-business reporting, and typical fee ranges cited in Bangladeshi business media. **Please verify exact current figures directly with a local accountant/lawyer in Khulna or by visiting KCC (Khulna City Corporation) offices** — fees can also change with each fiscal year's budget.

---

## 1. Trade License (Khulna City Corporation)

- **What it is:** The core, mandatory license to legally operate any shop/business — required before almost everything else (banks, other registrations often ask for it first).
- **Where:** Khulna City Corporation (KCC) — apply with NID, shop rental agreement, photos.
- **Estimated cost:** Small food/retail shops in Bangladesh typically pay **500–3,000 BDT/year** in license fee, though actual city corporation schedules include multiple line items (license fee + signboard tax + VAT component + other small charges) that can add up. One real trade license example found for a small enterprise showed a total fee around **850 BDT** for a full year ([Scribd example](https://www.scribd.com/document/850173855/tread-license)) — but a food/beverage shop may sit in a higher fee bracket than a general retail example.
- **Renewal:** Annual — budget for this every year, not just at setup.
- **My estimate for your case: ~1,000–3,000 BDT for the first year**, but confirm the actual bracket for a food/beverage business with KCC directly, since food businesses are sometimes charged more than general retail due to additional inspection/oversight.

## 2. TIN (Tax Identification Number)

- **What it is:** Required for any registered business owner; also needed to apply for other registrations (IRC, VAT/turnover tax enlistment).
- **Where:** National Board of Revenue (NBR) — online application via e-TIN portal.
- **Estimated cost: Free.** Registering for a TIN itself has no government fee — it's a simple online registration. (Any cost you incur would only be if you pay an accountant/agent to help file it for you, typically a small service fee.)

## 3. VAT / Turnover Tax Enlistment

- **What it is:** Depending on your expected annual turnover, you either register for full 15% VAT (turnover above 3 crore BDT/year) or the simpler **4% turnover tax** (for businesses with turnover between 50 lakh and 3 crore BDT/year) ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.global/en/insights/indirect-tax-guide/indirect-tax---Bangladesh/); [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/apac/bangladesh)).
- **For your shop:** Given your ~6 lakh BDT startup capital and modest first-year sales, you'll almost certainly fall under the **turnover tax** regime, not full VAT — much simpler compliance.
- **Estimated cost:** Registration itself is generally **free/nominal** — the real "cost" is the ongoing 4% turnover tax you pay on actual sales revenue once registered, not a flat setup fee. Budget for this as a percentage of revenue, not a one-time cost.

## 4. BSTI (Bangladesh Standards and Testing Institution) — Product/Quality Certification

- **What it is:** BSTI enforces mandatory quality certification for many food & beverage product categories in Bangladesh. Whether **your specific bubble tea drinks/ingredients require a BSTI CM (Compulsory Marks) license** depends on exact product classification — this is genuinely unclear from public sources for a made-to-order beverage shop (as opposed to a packaged/bottled product manufacturer).
- **What I could confirm:** BSTI does enforce this seriously — companies have been fined lakhs of taka for operating without required certification ([TBS News: ACI Godrej, Aftab Feed fined Tk14.8 lakh](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/aci-godrej-aftab-feed-among-seven-firms-fined-tk148-lakh-lacking-bsti-certification)), so this isn't a rule you want to guess on.
- **My honest recommendation:** A small takeaway shop making fresh drinks on-site (not packaging/selling bottled products) **likely falls under general food-safety/health-permit requirements rather than BSTI's product certification regime**, which is more geared toward manufactured/packaged goods. But **please confirm this directly with BSTI's Khulna regional office or a local food-business consultant** before assuming you're exempt — the cost of being wrong (fines) is much higher than the cost of a confirmation visit.
- **If required, typical BSTI CM license costs** for small businesses have been reported in the range of a few thousand to low tens-of-thousands of BDT depending on product category and testing requirements — but I don't have a confirmed figure specific to beverage retail, so treat any number here as unverified.

## 5. Food/Health & Sanitary Certificate

- **What it is:** Local health authority sign-off that your food prep space meets basic sanitary standards — commonly required alongside the trade license for any food-serving business.
- **Estimated cost: Low, typically a few hundred to ~1,000–2,000 BDT**, though this varies by local authority and isn't consistently documented for Khulna specifically.

## 6. Fire Safety Certificate

- **What it is:** Fire service clearance certificate, commonly required for restaurants/food businesses in Bangladesh as part of the standard licensing bundle ([Restorapos guide](https://restorapos.com/blog/how-to-open-a-restaurant-in-bangladesh); [TBS News on restaurant permit process](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/want-restaurant-permit-brace-red-tape-nightmare-808262)).
- **Estimated cost:** Not clearly published, but generally a modest fee (likely low thousands of BDT) plus the cost of actually installing a fire extinguisher and meeting basic safety requirements (already budgeted in your equipment list).

## 7. Partnership Firm Registration (RJSC) — *if you choose to formally register the partnership*

- **What it is:** Optional but recommended once the business is real — registers your partnership deed with the Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms (RJSC), giving it legal standing beyond just an informal family agreement.
- **Estimated cost:** RJSC fees scale with capital and involve both a registration fee and stamp duty on the partnership deed. For a business at your scale (6 lakh BDT total capital), this would likely fall in the **lowest fee bracket** — but exact current RJSC fee schedules for partnership firms (as opposed to limited companies) weren't clearly available in my search. **This needs direct confirmation from RJSC or a local lawyer** — likely a few thousand BDT total including stamp duty, but treat this as a rough guess only.
- **Note:** As discussed earlier, many small Bangladeshi family businesses start as an informal partnership (deed signed privately, one person as the "official" sole proprietor for trade license purposes) and only formalize with RJSC once scaling — this is a legitimate, common path if you want to minimize upfront registration costs and complexity.

## ⚠️ My strongest recommendation on this topic

Licensing/compliance costs are the area where my research has the **least reliable data** of everything we've covered so far — government fee schedules for Bangladesh are genuinely difficult to find in clean, current, English-language form. Rather than budgeting precisely off the numbers above, I'd suggest:

1. **Set aside a compliance budget of ~20,000–30,000 BDT** as a safe buffer for your first year (covering license fees + a possible small consultant/accountant fee to help you navigate the process correctly).
2. **Visit or call Khulna City Corporation's trade license office directly** — this is the single most important call to make, since the trade license unlocks/is required for most other registrations.
3. **Ask specifically about BSTI applicability for a made-to-order beverage shop** — this is the biggest compliance unknown, and getting it wrong risks fines, not just inconvenience.
4. Consider a **one-time consultation with a local business/tax consultant in Khulna** (often a few thousand BDT) — this is genuinely one of the best-value spends in your whole startup budget, since it replaces guesswork with confirmed local requirements.
