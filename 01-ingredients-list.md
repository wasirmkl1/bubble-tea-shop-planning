# Bubble Tea Shop — Full Ingredients List (A–Z)

Organized by category. Quantities are placeholders to be refined once your menu is finalized and daily sales volume is estimated.

## 1. Tea Bases
- Black tea (loose leaf or tea bags) — for classic milk tea
- Green tea — for jasmine/green milk tea, matcha-adjacent drinks
- Oolong tea — premium base, common in authentic Taiwanese-style shops
- Jasmine green tea — very popular base
- Thai tea mix (if doing Thai-style milk tea)
- Matcha powder (culinary grade, not ceremonial — cheaper, works fine for drinks)

## 2. Milk / Creamer
- Non-dairy creamer powder (most bubble tea shops use this — cheaper, longer shelf life, consistent taste, and common in authentic Taiwanese recipes)
- Evaporated milk or condensed milk (for richer classic milk tea)
- Fresh milk (optional, for a "fresh milk" premium line — shorter shelf life, needs refrigeration discipline)
- Oat milk / plant milk (optional, if you want a vegan-friendly option later)

## 3. Sweeteners / Syrups
- White sugar syrup (simple syrup, made in-house — cheap, no import needed)
- Brown sugar syrup / caramelized brown sugar syrup (the "brown sugar boba" trend — can make in-house or buy syrup)
- Honey (for honey-based drinks)
- Fructose syrup (commercial liquid sweetener — very common in professional bubble tea shops for consistent sweetness control, dispensed via a fructose pump)
- Flavored syrups: taro, mango, lychee, strawberry, passionfruit, peach, grape — these are the ones most worth importing since they're hard to source authentically in Bangladesh

## 4. Toppings ("Boba" / Chewy Additions)
- Tapioca pearls (black boba) — the core, essential import item
- Popping boba (fruit-flavored bursting pearls: mango, strawberry, lychee, passionfruit)
- Crystal boba / white pearls (clear, chewier, less common but a nice differentiator)
- Grass jelly
- Coconut jelly / nata de coco
- Aloe vera bits
- Red bean (azuki)
- Pudding (egg pudding cubes — trendy topping)
- Cheese foam mix (for "cheese tea" style drinks — trendy with teens)

## 5. Fruit & Flavor Concentrates
- Mango puree/concentrate
- Passionfruit puree/concentrate
- Strawberry puree/concentrate
- Lychee syrup/concentrate
- Peach syrup/concentrate
- Lemon/calamansi concentrate (for fruit teas)
- Taro powder (for taro milk tea — very popular flavor)
- Thai tea flavor powder

## 6. Packaging & Consumables
- Plastic cups (16oz/22oz sizes common — pick 1-2 sizes to start)
- Cup lids (dome lids if doing whipped cream/cheese foam toppings)
- Sealing film rolls (for the cup sealing machine — must match your sealer brand/size)
- Wide bubble tea straws (thick enough for tapioca pearls to pass through)
- Straw wrappers (optional but adds a "premium" feel)
- Take-away carrier bags/cup holders (2-cup, 4-cup carriers for group orders)
- Napkins
- Branded stickers/labels for cups (do locally — cheap and high branding impact)

## 7. Ice & Water
- Ice (either self-made via ice machine or bulk-bought daily from a local ice supplier — common in Bangladesh)
- Filtered/RO water (critical for taste consistency and food safety — invest in a decent water filter)

## 8. Cleaning & Hygiene Supplies
- Food-safe sanitizer
- Dish soap, scrubbers, cleaning cloths
- Hand sanitizer/handwash for staff
- Disposable gloves
- Hairnets/caps for staff (important for a food business, also builds customer trust)

## Which of these should actually be imported from China vs. sourced locally/from Dhaka?

**Worth importing from China (authentic quality really differs):**
- Tapioca pearls (black boba)
- Popping boba (fruit flavors)
- Flavored syrups (taro, brown sugar, fruit concentrates)
- Non-dairy creamer powder
- Grass jelly, coconut jelly, pudding mix

**Better sourced locally or via existing Dhaka wholesalers (faster, no import hassle, good enough quality):**
- Cups, lids, straws, sealing film (unless you want fully custom branded cups — even then, many China suppliers on Alibaba do custom printing at low MOQs)
- Sugar, ice, water, cleaning supplies
- Fresh milk (if used)
- Basic loose-leaf black/green tea (decent quality available in BD, though Chinese/Taiwanese tea will taste more "authentic")

This split matters because it keeps your first shipment small, focused on the ingredients that actually make the biggest taste/authenticity difference — not on bulky low-value items like cups that add shipping cost without adding "wow factor."

### Important notes on this estimate
- **This does not include your equipment costs** (cup sealer, dispensers, etc. — see `02-shop-equipment-list.md`), only consumable ingredients.
- **Freight cost swings a lot** based on shipping method: sea freight (slower, cheaper per kg, better for 100kg+ loads) vs. air express (faster, much more expensive per kg, only worth it for a very first small test order). For ~120kg, sea freight consolidated via a forwarder is likely more economical — get 2-3 quotes from China→Bangladesh freight forwarders before committing.
- **Customs/duty depends on the exact HS code** each product is classified under — some food ingredient categories also require a BSTI clearance certificate at the port. Confirm with your C&F agent before shipping, not after.
- Consider **starting even smaller** (e.g., a half-batch, ~60kg total) purely to test product quality, your recipes, and real customer demand before committing to a larger repeat order — first orders from a new supplier always carry some quality-consistency risk.
- Once you're placing **repeat monthly/bi-monthly orders**, per-kg prices typically drop as you qualify for better MOQ tiers and negotiate directly with the supplier — so this first-batch cost is likely your **highest per-unit cost**, not a representative ongoing cost.
