# Price-Per-Cup Cost Estimate & Suggested Menu Pricing

This builds on the cost data already gathered in `01-ingredients-list.md` and `02-shop-equipment-list.md`. It breaks down **exactly what one cup costs you to make** (ingredients + packaging + overhead), then suggests a selling price range based on standard beverage-business margins.

⚠️ **As with the earlier documents, treat all numbers here as a planning framework, not a final price.** The formulas and structure are solid — but you should plug in your own real numbers once you have them (actual rent, actual staff wages, your real menu) and compare against what nearby snack/juice/coffee shops in Khulna charge for a similar treat, since that tells you what your target customers (students) are actually willing to pay.

## Step 2: Overhead cost per cup (rent, staff, utilities)

Direct ingredient cost is only part of the picture — rent, staff wages, and utilities have to be paid whether you sell 20 cups or 100 cups a day, so they need to be spread ("allocated") across your expected monthly volume.

**Assumptions used below (please replace with your real numbers once known):**
- Monthly sales volume: **2,400 cups/month** (~80 cups/day — a reasonable target for a takeaway spot near schools/coaching centers)
- Rent: **18,000 BDT/month** (mid-point of the earlier placeholder range — confirm with real quotes)
- Staff: **2 people at ~10,000 BDT/month each = 20,000 BDT/month** (family members drawing a modest wage, or hired staff)
- Utilities (electricity, gas, water): **6,500 BDT/month**
- Misc overhead (cleaning supplies, small maintenance, marketing): **5,000 BDT/month**

| Overhead item | Monthly cost (BDT) | Cost per cup (at 2,400 cups/month) |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | 18,000 | 7.5 |
| Staff wages | 20,000 | 8.3 |
| Utilities | 6,500 | 2.7 |
| Misc/marketing | 5,000 | 2.1 |
| **Total overhead per cup** | | **~20.6 BDT** |

**Important:** this number is highly sensitive to your actual sales volume. If you only sell 40 cups/day (1,200/month) instead of 80, your overhead per cup roughly **doubles to ~41 BDT** — this is exactly why hitting a reasonable daily volume matters so much in the beverage business. Low volume is often the real reason small cafes struggle, even when the drinks themselves are priced fine.

## Step 4: Suggested selling price at different margin targets

Beverage businesses (cafes, juice bars, tea shops) typically target **60–75% gross margin** on drinks — meaning ingredients+packaging+overhead should be roughly 25–40% of the selling price. Here's what that translates to:

| Target gross margin | Formula | Suggested price per cup |
|---|---|---|
| 60% margin | cost ÷ 0.40 | ~137 BDT |
| 65% margin | cost ÷ 0.35 | ~157 BDT |
| 70% margin | cost ÷ 0.30 | ~183 BDT |
| 75% margin | cost ÷ 0.25 | ~220 BDT |

### My honest read on this

A price of **150–180 BDT per cup** looks like a realistic starting range for Khulna — high enough to hit a healthy margin, but still an affordable "treat" price for a student buying with pocket money (roughly comparable to a fast-food combo item or a couple of street snacks). Going above ~200 BDT risks pricing out your core teenage audience in a secondary city like Khulna, even though Dhaka mall-based chains like Chatime can charge more given their higher-income mall foot traffic.

**A tiered menu often works best in practice:**
- Basic milk teas: ~130–150 BDT (drives volume, gets people in the habit of visiting)
- Specialty/fruit/popping boba drinks: ~170–200 BDT (your higher-margin, "treat yourself" tier)

## ⚠️ What you should verify before finalizing prices

1. **Local willingness-to-pay is the biggest unknown.** I don't have confirmed real-world Bangladesh bubble tea pricing (Chatime/ChaJoy Dhaka prices weren't available in my search results with confirmed BDT figures). Please visit or check the Facebook/Instagram pages of Chatime, ChaJoy, or Ai-CHA outlets to see their actual menu prices — this is your single best benchmark, even though they're in Dhaka/Chattogram malls with likely higher price tolerance than Khulna.
2. **Test with your family/friends first** — ask what they'd realistically pay for a cup near a Khulna coaching center, before locking in prices.
3. **Your real rent, staff wage, and volume numbers will shift the overhead-per-cup figure meaningfully** — rerun Step 2 once you have actual quotes.
4. **Wastage isn't included above** — spoiled milk, over-cooked pearls, mistaken orders. Budget an extra ~5–8% on top of direct costs to be safe.
5. Consider **starting slightly lower** (e.g. 130–150 BDT flat across the menu) for your first 1–2 months to build a customer base and word-of-mouth near the schools, then adjust once you see real demand patterns.
