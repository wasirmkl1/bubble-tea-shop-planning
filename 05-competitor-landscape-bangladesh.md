## 🚨 Major implication: my earlier price estimate was too LOW

In `03-price-per-cup-estimate.md` I suggested a selling price of **150–180 BDT/cup**, flagged as unverified because I couldn't find real Bangladesh prices. **Now I have them, and the real market is priced considerably higher:**

| Brand | Regular size | Large size |
|---|---|---|
| Koi Thé | 220 | 280 |
| Chamichi | 300 | 420 |
| Gotcha | 400 (500ml) | 449 (700ml) |

**Dhaka market range: roughly Tk 220–450 per cup.** Note the 2023 article itself describes boba as "a rather overpriced cup" — so even locals perceive these as premium-priced.

### What this means for your Khulna pricing

- Your estimated **cost per cup (~55 BDT)** against a Dhaka price of 220–450 implies these brands are running **very high gross margins (75–88%)** — much higher than the 60–75% I assumed.
- **Khulna is not Dhaka.** These are mall/upscale-neighbourhood outlets in the capital serving higher-income customers. Khulna students will not pay Tk 400.
- However, it does mean **my 150–180 BDT suggestion was likely too conservative.** A range of **180–250 BDT** now looks more defensible — meaningfully undercutting Dhaka premium brands (a genuine selling point: "authentic boba without Dhaka prices") while capturing much better margin than my original estimate.
- Suggested tiering to reconsider:
  - Classic milk teas: **~180–200 BDT**
  - Specialty/fruit/popping boba: **~220–250 BDT**

At 200 BDT/cup against a 55 BDT cost, your gross margin is ~72% — healthy and sustainable, while still visibly cheaper than every Dhaka brand.

## Strategic takeaways for your Khulna shop

1. **Khulna remains unserved.** Every confirmed brand operates only in **Dhaka or Chattogram**. No bubble tea brand found in Khulna — your first-mover thesis holds.
2. **Popping boba is a real differentiator even in Dhaka.** Chamichi's own founder says popping boba pearls are "rarely found in Dhaka." If you import them (already in your plan per `01-ingredients-list.md`), you'd be offering something even the capital struggles to supply.
3. **Imported-ingredient authenticity is the premium positioning play.** Koi Thé imports everything but water from Taiwan; Gotcha imports brown sugar and Taiwanese cream cheese powder. Your plan to import authentic ingredients from China puts you in the same quality tier — worth saying out loud in your marketing.
4. **Cheese foam / brown sugar are the proven winners.** Gotcha's Brown Sugar Cheese Tea is described as the drink that made the brand popular. Both are already on your ingredients list — prioritise nailing these two recipes.
5. **Sugar/ice customisation is table stakes.** ChaJoy explicitly offers it and it's appreciated by reviewers. Cheap to offer, strong perceived value.
6. **Price gap = your wedge.** You can position as "Dhaka-quality authentic boba at Khulna prices" and still earn a strong margin.
